Copenhagen or Stockholm: where does a marketing professional keep more?

A like-for-like comparison of monthly disposable income — net salary after tax, minus rent and essential living costs — for junior, mid-level and senior marketing roles. Everything is converted to Danish kroner (DKK) at the current rate.

Prepared 16 Jul 2026 Currency 1 SEK = 0.678 DKK Tax year 2026 Scope single adult, no children

THE BOTTOM LINEIt depends on seniority — and rent decides it

Denmark pays marketing staff more in cash terms, but Stockholm's rent is dramatically cheaper relative to pay. The two forces cross over somewhere between mid-level and senior.

Junior — winner
Stockholm +1,258 kr
6,191 kr vs 4,933 kr left per month (median rent). Stockholm keeps 31% of net pay vs 22%.
Mid-level — winner
Stockholm +345 kr
13,254 kr vs 12,909 kr. A narrow win — and it holds across low, median and high rent.
Senior — winner
Copenhagen +1,406 kr
21,097 kr vs 19,691 kr. High Danish salaries finally outrun the rent gap.
One-line answer: For the typical marketing professional — junior to mid-level, the bulk of the workforce — Stockholm leaves more disposable income each month and a larger share of net pay after necessities. Copenhagen only pulls ahead at senior/leadership level, where markedly higher Danish salaries overtake its higher rents. Across every level, Stockholm retains a higher percentage of net income because housing costs so much less relative to salary.

02 · TAXFrom gross to net

Denmark front-loads an 8% labour-market contribution and then layers municipal + state tax; Sweden leans on a flat municipal rate softened by a generous work tax credit (jobbskatteavdrag). The net effect: Sweden taxes these salary levels noticeably less, which offsets part of Denmark's higher gross pay.

Denmark — Copenhagen resident, 2026

  • AM-bidrag 8% of gross, taken first
  • Municipal tax (København) 23.39% — the country's lowest
  • Bundskat 12.01% · Mellemskat 7.5% over 641,200 kr/yr · Topskat 15% over 777,900 kr/yr
  • Personal allowance 54,100 kr · Employment deduction 12.75% (max 63,300 kr) · Job deduction max 3,100 kr
  • No church tax assumed (would add ≈ 0.8%)

Sweden — Stockholm resident, 2026

  • Municipal + regional tax 30.55% (Stockholm 17.90% + Region 12.65%)
  • State tax 20% only above 643,000 kr taxable (≈ 55,000 kr/month)
  • Basic deduction (grundavdrag) ≈ 16,500 kr at these incomes
  • Work tax credit (jobbskatteavdrag) ≈ 39,900 kr/yr — its cap; 2026 removed the high-income phase-out
  • No church fee assumed (member fee ≈ 0.7%); funeral fee 0.29% is negligible

07 · CONCLUSIONThe verdict

For most marketing professionals, Stockholm leaves more money in your pocket. A junior keeps about 1,258 kr more per month (≈ 26% more disposable income), and a mid-level manager keeps a slim 345 kr more. The reason is housing: Stockholm's net salaries are lower, but its rent is roughly 3,000 kr/month cheaper and its taxes are lighter, which more than compensates until pay gets high.

Only at senior level does Copenhagen win, by about 1,406 kr/month: Danish leadership salaries are high enough — and taxed below the punishing top brackets at 60 k/month — that they finally overtake Stockholm's cheaper cost base.

On share of net pay retained after necessities, Stockholm wins at every level (31% vs 22% junior, 49% vs 43% mid, 59% vs 55% senior) — a cleaner signal of day-to-day financial comfort than the absolute figure.

If you must pick one city: choose Stockholm for a junior or mid-career marketing job — more disposable income and more breathing room. Choose Copenhagen if you're targeting a senior / head-of-marketing role, where the higher salary wins out. The difference either way is modest (roughly 300–1,400 kr/month), so lifestyle, career opportunity and language should carry real weight alongside the money.

METHODAssumptions

  • Profile: a single adult, no children, renting alone, employed full-time in a private-sector marketing role, tax resident in the city.
  • Exchange rate: 1 SEK = 0.678 DKK (mid-market, 16 Jul 2026). A ±3% move in the rate shifts Stockholm's DKK figures by a few hundred kroner but does not flip any level's verdict.
  • Salaries are average gross monthly figures per seniority band, cash before tax. They are estimates from salary aggregators and official statistics, not a single authoritative table — the true market spread is wide.
  • Occupational pension is excluded on both sides. Danish labour-market pension and Swedish tjänstepension are employer/collective schemes that don't reach the monthly current account, so leaving them out keeps the take-home comparison clean and symmetric.
  • Church tax / church fee excluded (non-member assumed) in both cities.
  • Tax uses 2026 statutory rules: Danish figures computed directly; Swedish figures calibrated to a Stockholm 2026 net-salary calculator (grundavdrag ≈ 16,500 kr, jobbskatteavdrag ≈ 39,900 kr/yr at its post-reform cap).
  • Rent is the median asking rent for a one-bedroom ~10–15 min from the centre by metro. Stockholm uses the second-hand/open market (realistic for a new arrival), not regulated first-hand contracts.
  • Live per-listing scraping was not available; rent medians are calibrated to aggregated 2026 portal distributions rather than 20 individually captured listings. This is the analysis's main data limitation.
  • Essentials cover groceries, utilities, internet, mobile, and a monthly transit pass — the recurring mandatory costs of living. Discretionary spending is by definition part of "disposable income," not deducted.
